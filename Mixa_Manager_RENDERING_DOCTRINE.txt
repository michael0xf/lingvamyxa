Mixa Manager - the rendering doctrine
Application-wide. Not a preference, not a default to be overridden locally.

Fixed by the language owner on 2026-09-09. It lives in its own document rather
than inside BACKEND_SEAM.txt because it governs the WHOLE application: whoever
later writes image output, a viewer, a zoom control or a new layer must find it
without reading a document about backends.


1. The law

    A fraction is how you ARRIVE at an answer, not what you draw with.

Compute sizes and spacings in fractional arithmetic, then find INTEGER POSITIONS
AND INTEGER VALUES and draw there. No blurring, no fractional resize of anything
drawn.

Note the scope precisely, because getting it wrong once already cost a detour:
the rule is about POSITIONS AND VALUES. It does not require RATIOS between
things to be whole numbers. An earlier version of this document invented that
extra requirement, which is not part of the doctrine.


2. Why it is a prohibition and not advice

Most text windows today look wrong: smeared glyphs, uneven stems, box drawing
that does not meet. That is not incompetence, it is what standard scaling
machinery does BY DEFAULT when nobody forbids it. Fractional scaling, sub-pixel
positioning and smoothing are the defaults almost everywhere, so a project that
merely prefers sharpness loses it one reasonable-looking call at a time.


3. Fonts are never resized

Take the font at a size the system actually has and use it pixel for pixel.
Never scale one to reach a wanted size.

The upper layer wants roughly 1.5x the text cell, so take the AVAILABLE size
NEAREST to that. 8x16 might pair with 11x22, a factor of 1.375. That is correct
and finished - the discreteness the doctrine asks for is that the size is a real
one the system offers, not one we computed.

The two grids therefore do NOT generally align: upper cell boundaries will fall
inside text cells rather than on their edges. Accepted. Forcing alignment would
mean either scaling a font, which is forbidden, or refusing sizes that are
closer to what is wanted, which trades the thing we want for a property we do
not need.

A larger window shows MORE letters, never larger ones. Resize is lazy and
discrete: cell size fixed, cell count changes.


4. Images

Two different cases. Do not apply one rule to the other.

    IN THE INTERFACE. A small image, if one is ever needed, is placed exactly
    like a glyph: 1 to 1, no zoom at all. It is not scaled to fit anything.

    IN THE IMAGE VIEWER. A dedicated viewer, needed for browsing files, may fit
    an image to any window size by simple rules: proportional scaling, centred
    in the window. This is a viewer showing a picture, not the interface drawing
    itself, and ordinary fitting is right there.

The distinction is the point. The prohibition protects the INTERFACE, where
everything is cells and glyphs and a fraction always shows. A photo viewer is
not the interface.


5. Relation to other documents

BACKEND_SEAM.txt sections 9 and 10 apply this to the backend seam: cell metrics
reported by open, glyph rasterization at native size, and the two cell grids.
Those are instances. This document is the rule.
