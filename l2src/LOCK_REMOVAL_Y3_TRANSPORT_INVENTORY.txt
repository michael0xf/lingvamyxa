--- Production sites

lmx_message.lm1:952-990ish, lmx_msg_host_drain (push at 987, `lmx_msg_
  q_push(@ rt\transport, @ rt\transport_tail, n)`, inside a loop re-
  staging every item host_post admitted into R0's own ingress inbox):
  today, R0's between-turns maintenance takes its own ingress queue
  (under R0's mailbox monitor) and re-stages each item onto rt\
  transport (a second, sequential hold of the same monitor) for pump
  to relay later. Under Y3: the queue itself goes; this loop calls
  lmx_msg_admit_one(rt, n) directly on each item instead of q_push,
  collapsing "take from R0's ingress, queue for later, pump relays it"
  into "take from R0's ingress, admit it now" -- one hold sequence
  (R0's monitor, released, then the destination's own monitor) instead
  of two holds separated by an unbounded delay until the next pump.

lmx_message.lm1:1735-1782ish, lmx_msg_end_turn (push at 1780, inside
  the loop relaying the just-finished Message's own outbox): today, a
  Message's own outbox (what it sent during its turn, taken and
  released under its own mailbox monitor) is relayed onto rt\transport,
  a second sequential hold of R0's monitor, for pump to deliver later.
  Under Y3: each outbox envelope is admitted directly into its own
  resolved destination's mailbox in this same loop, the same
  collapse as host_drain's.

lmx_message.lm1:1975-2003ish, lmx_msg_post_dead (push at 2001, the one
  status-envelope site: builds a node with `node\kind: c.LMX_MSG_KIND_

  its own, since its caller lmx_msg_fail already holds no mailbox
  monitor at this point): today, a Message's death is announced to a
  watcher by staging a KIND_DEAD envelope on rt\transport for pump to
  deliver. Under Y3: admit_one(rt, node) directly, same collapse; this

  application send, named separately since the ticket asks for it by

lmx_message.lm1:1858-1888ish, lmx_msg_pump (pop and relay, 1878-1887,
  its own comment "the take is under R0's mailbox monitor and is left
  before admit_one takes MAIL on the destination"): today, the one
  reader of rt\transport, draining it under R0's monitor (released
  before each admit_one call, re-taken for the next iteration) and
  admitting each item into its real destination. Under Y3: this
  function's own queue-draining body goes; per the design doc's own
  note, pump itself stays PUBLIC (lmx_message.h:236) because ~61
  fixtures call it expecting a real function to exist at that name and
  signature, but its body becomes a no-op returning LMX_MSG_OK (nothing
  left to drain, since host_drain/end_turn/post_dead now admit
  directly) -- the two-step "stage, then pump moves it" shape the
  fixtures pin becomes "already delivered; pump finds nothing to do."
  This is Y3's own design decision to make explicit when the code
  lands, not answered here: either pump becomes this permanent no-op
  (keeping every fixture's own call compiling and passing, since
  inbox_n would already read the delivered count before pump runs) or
  the ~61 call sites are edited out along with their own before/after
  inbox_n pattern. The design doc's own words ("pump is public ... so
  deleting the queue changes observable behaviour") read as leaning
  toward the no-op, not the edit, but does not rule it -- flagged as
  the one open point for Y3's own ticket.

lmx_message.lm1:1010-1023ish, lmx_msg_runtime_delete (frees the queue
  at 1020-1022, `lmx_msg_copy_free(rt\transport)` then both pointers
  to 0, the host-only teardown path): today, the final cleanup of

--- Fixtures pinning pump's two-step shape (the numeric/observable pin
    the queue's deletion shifts)

The design doc's own canonical example, verified directly: l2src/tests/
lmx_msg_delivery_selftest.lm1:81, `check(lmx_msg_inbox_n(rt, child) = 0
&& lmx_msg_pump(rt) = c.LMX_MSG_OK && lmx_msg_inbox_n(rt, child) = 0,
"the unpublished delivery never reaches C")` -- and the file's own next
case (a successful send) checks inbox_n = 0 before pump, then = 1
after. This is the shape all 61 lmx_msg_pump( call sites share in
outline (not each one individually re-read this pass, given the
count): a send/relay is asserted STAGED but not yet delivered
(inbox_n = 0, or an equivalent not-yet-there check), pump is called,
then the delivery is asserted present (inbox_n = 1, or the specific
value expected). Under Y3, if pump becomes a permanent no-op (the
design doc's own leaning, not ruled), these checks do not need editing
at all -- inbox_n would already read the post-admission value at the
first check, since admission is now immediate, and the pump() call in
the middle still returns LMX_MSG_OK, doing nothing, harmlessly. The 61
sites are therefore not necessarily 61 edits; they are 61 sites whose
own correctness depends on Y3's single design decision (no-op pump
versus edited-out call), not 61 independent numeric pins the way the
slots-list rt\n pins were for S6-2. Flagged as the central finding
this ticket's own "numeric pins the deletion shifts" question turns
on, not resolved here.
