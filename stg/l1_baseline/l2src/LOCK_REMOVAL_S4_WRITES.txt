LOCK_REMOVAL_S4_WRITES: every write into an LmxMsg row (LOCK_REMOVAL_FIELDS.txt at
23cda3c0) made by a thread other than that Message's own lane, at integration
c063fd00, against spec 19.28.R2.2's "one arena, one lane, one writer" clarification
(2026-09-14) and its ownership clauses (sonnet/s4-writes, 2026-09-15, doc-only,
no machine).

Scope: LmxMsg rows only (not the ExecBind/BindWait/Runtime/CtxPack scaffolding --
those are the S3/M files' subject). Excluded per the ticket: the mailbox rows
(inbox, inbox_tail, mail; LOCK_REMOVAL_Y_SITES.txt has them) and the three
LmxMsg rows the FIELDS table already stages at S3 (closing, ready, exec_live).
Arena-internal rows (blocks, ranges, eternal_ranges, method_ranges, graph,
roots) are folded into the creation site below rather than listed one by one:
every one of them is set only at lmx_msg_create_prepare, before the child has a
lane, or mutated during the owning Message's own turn (the serial-turns safety
claim of spec 19.28.R2.2 itself) -- no separate foreign-lane write of any
arena-internal row was found outside creation.

Note on wording below: the ticket's four answers (the parent's supervision
cells, the control flags clause, a sender's delivery into the target's own
queue, or unaccounted-for) are spelled out below using paraphrase in this
header and in every explanatory sentence, reserving the exact tagged spelling
for the single "permits:" line each site carries, so a grep for those four
spellings counts sites, not prose.

Clauses, from Lingvamyxa_spec.txt:11507-11526. Clause one: a Message's own
lane owns its arena, scheduler record/cursor, its own ready clear, its mailbox
except delivery in, native_users and handoff-safe marks, success, its own
running clear at end_turn. Clause two: the parent owns the supervision cells
about its direct children "wherever they sit physically" -- committed,
tracked, the last-heard time, the execution mapping written at bind, the
family chain (first/last child, siblings' links, the parent link at link,
unlink and supervision handoff), and the settle writes (storage moved into
the parent, the child's graph and init at settle). Clause three: control
flags, one designated cross-lane writer each -- the parent clears a child's
running and sets its stop flag; a sender writes the destination's readiness
flag when delivering into its queue; the lane that takes a Message's turn
clears that Message's own readiness flag. Clause four, separately named in the
spec but not among the ticket's three answers: "Primitives: mailbox delivery
under its lock, reference counts, wait signals" -- where a site needs this
fourth clause instead of the ticket's three, this file says so plainly and
tags the site's own line as unaccounted-for by the ticket's own list.

--- running: checked, no site to list

Checked explicitly since the control-flags clause names it (the parent clears
a child's running to signal stop): the only write call site found is the
atomic store inside lmx_msg_end_turn (lmx_message.lm1:1759), where the target
is the ending Message's own record -- its own clause-one running clear at
end_turn, not a foreign-lane write. A parent's stop reaches the target only by
sending a STOP-kind envelope (a sender's delivery into the target's own
queue), which the target then acts on during its own turn -- a self-write, not
a direct field write from the parent's lane. The spec's sentence describes the
logical effect; the code implements it through delivery, not a direct
cross-lane store. No site is listed for this field; recorded here so the
absence reads as checked, not missed.

--- Sites needing a new lock, wait or signal to keep: zero found

Every writer above already runs under the executor's own shared-table lock for
the moment it reaches into another Message's fields -- the same lock already
used throughout create, link, settle and bind today, not something any site
here would need to newly add.
