STAGE 5 (d) TEST MIGRATION: the stepping calls of main move into R0's turn (0c, 2026-09-14)

The rule, per e2's ruling and d6's tickets
  After d3 a step is the parent's act on the parent's lane:
    - run_child_turn, map_child and sched_step need the parent's turn;
    - exec_ui_step needs R0's turn (d2 makes the UI lane R0's child).
  Between root_turns the host does only drive, host_drain and, until (f), runtime_delete.
  Until d3 the authority stays dual (the parent's turn, or the host outside any turn), so every
  migrated test is green under both authorities.
  Tests take the nested shape:
    - main calls lmx_msg_root_turn;
    - R0's turn steps its child;
    - each intermediate parent is bound from main to turn_step_child, only around the step
      (bind's authority is not one of the four flipped APIs);
    - the stepped child keeps its own test turn.
  A parent whose own turn does real work gets a composite turn (turn_sched_step, turn_map_child).
  Ruled out: no ancestor rule (decision 18), and no flattened fixtures.

The shared helper
  turn_step_child has the same text in every migrated test. Its cell holds [0] the child's
  address, written before the root_turn, and [1] that step's status, written by the turn.
  The chain runs the helper at more than one level, so a single status global would be
  overwritten by the outer step. Each test's chain helper returns root_turn's status when the
  turn is refused, else the first level's status that is not OK, else the stepped child's.

Findings
  1. In the nested shape, run_child_turn's tail settle runs inside the parent's turn
     (exec.c: `if (par != 0U && lmx_msg_exec_holding_turn(rt, par) != 0) parent_settle(par)`).
     scenario36 migrated goes red at "FAIL B leaves its native activation, handoff-safe" and "FAIL P
     adopts B's retained state" (49 checks, 2 failures). A print after B's cancel step gives
     "DIAG pst=0 b_exists=0 handoff_b=0 child_n_p=1 adopted_n_p=17": P's settle adopted B at the
     step, so main's explicit adopt finds nothing. With turn_step_child returning 0 instead of
     end_turn(rt, who, 1), the two FAILs are the same, so the helper's end_turn on P is not the
     cause. Routed to the lead as d1c. Checks and lifecycle sentences are unchanged.
  2. The first d3 flip text refused every L2 library open: run_entry_turn goes through
     run_child_turn from the host. Under that flip, sched_record exits 3 in both variants with
     "lmx: library open failed: l2_uB1771DBC664A1B11 lmx_sched_record_new". Answered by d6: d3
     gives the bootstrap its own path. The g_entry_bootstrap flag above stands in for it in
     scratch only.
  3. Role history (exec selftest, batch 6). The fixture allocated the RETAIN temp in C's arena
     before G's step and rooted it afterwards. Migrated, G's step runs inside C's turn, and that
     turn's end_turn collects C's unrooted ranges, so the temp was freed. A reverted print gave
     "temp_in_c before_step=1 after_step=0 after_adopt=0 attach_temp=2". This is the runtime's
     rule (an unrooted range dies at its owner's turn end), not a gap. By d6's ruling the temp is
     now allocated after the step and adopt, just before its root_attach. Every assertion is
     unchanged; the combined check splits into "role history G->C", "role history temp" and
     "role history G->C attach".
  4. Shape (a). A cancelled or completed parent runs no turn body (exec.c run_one), so it cannot
     step its children. Until (f), in e2's order:
       - the steps a live parent can take run from R0's chain;
       - the host unbinds the child its parent cannot step;
       - drive's unbound close leaves that child handoff-ready (d1d, d897c16e), and the parent's
         dispose or adopt then settles it.
     family_handoff's G (parent C cancelled) is the first case. The held exec cases are next.
  5. The sched-cursor host case (exec selftest, batch 4) pinned the host path, which d3 removes, so
     it is deleted by d6's ruling. The line "exec wait: host parent step continues after its
     cursor: A, B, A, B" no longer prints. "exec wait: parent step in its own turn continues after
     its cursor: A, B, A, B" keeps the observation on P's own lane.
  6. The maintain case (exec selftest, batch 7). The orphan C's step goes R0 -> C (d6's reading:
     orphan_children re-roots a running child under R0, and d1b's parent_settle skips orphans).
     Its end-turn check read only find(c), find(p) and the slot count, and all three held with or
     without C's step; the first observer was the outside reclaim. A reverted print after the check
     gave "c_state=2 c_handoff=1" with the step and "c_state=0 c_handoff=0" without. By d6's ruling
     the check gains handoff_ready(c), a strengthening.

Measurement traps
  - run_msg_send_local, run_msg_family_handoff and run_l2_message_root git-archive the core at
    -CoreCommit (default HEAD) and read only the test from the working tree. A falsifier that
    mutates the core must be a local scratch commit, never pushed and reset afterwards.
    For those runners, a red-to-green across a core step needs no scratch commit: run the same
    test with -CoreCommit set to the step's merge parent, then to the tip (family_handoff:
    ce0bd65d, then f9a25865).
  - run_model_scenario36 and run_sched_record build the working tree.
  - A migration adds turns: the Message that now steps a child runs an end_turn, and that end_turn
    collects its unrooted ranges (finding 3). A fixture value made before the step and rooted after
    it dies in between.
  - A site falsifier must go red at the check that observes the step, not at a later one. If it goes
    red later, the case lacks an observer for that step (finding 6).
