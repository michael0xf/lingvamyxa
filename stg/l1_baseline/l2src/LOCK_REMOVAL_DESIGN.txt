WHAT THE LOCK DOES TODAY (from the inventories, not from a rule)
L1. Address resolution. A walk from rt->root through every child list turns an LmxMsgAddr into a record
    (msg_find_any_locked, lmx_msg_find, self_or_find). The lock keeps the tree still under the walk. This is 35 of
    exec.c's 41 silent 5.1 lines.
L2. The family tree's own writes: child_link, child_chain_remove, the orphan re-rooting, the root-list unlink in
    endp_try_retire and release_slot.
L3. The execution mapping's cells: exec_bind record fields, mapped, launching, the bind wait's generation.
L4. Control flags read and written across lanes: ready, closing, running, handoff_ready, native_users.
L5. Runtime-level lists: rt->slots/n, e->retire_head, e->reap_head, rt->transport, e->ui_lane.
L6. Wakes: exec_wake_locked signals every worker after any turn; exec_ready routes one wake.
L7. Storage moves at settle: adopt_mark, dispose_mark, transfer_adopted, transfer_graph.
L8. Test setters and counters.

STAGES (each: red first, the change, green; the full gate on its integration merge)
Every stage runs run_gates N of N, run_port_message (the executor selftest), run_model_scenario36 cold, and 57's lane
tripwire once it is measured. A stage that removes lock calls also runs the UAF hunt kit's load shapes for 165 clean
runs (the kit's number).

S0. The proof tool (57): the lane tripwire red on a lane write from a thread with no turn. It is the proof for S2-S5
    and lands first.

S1. Dead code and the order's direct deletions (no question open).
    - Delete adopt_push (exec.c 3628, unused; its only occurrence is its definition).
    - Red: none needed for dead code. The proof is the grep at 0 and the gates green.
    Pending Mikhail's answer, not in S1: lm2/own.lm2's unused native mutex/condition wrappers (9 lines).

S2. Capabilities instead of address lookups inside the runtime (L1), locking unchanged.
    Every call site of the spine's C1, C2 and C5 takes the record its caller already holds. The site no longer
    resolves an address: run_one and the context worker use the record the take handed them (C2); bind, map, unbind,
    settle and step use the parent's child pointer (C1); R0's sweep uses R0's child list (C5). C3 (send) and C4
    (liveness toward the parent) change in S4, where the capability's form is decided.
    - Red: a test-build counter in lmx_msg_find and msg_find_any_locked, reported per caller class. It runs the
      executor selftest and the model tests, and asserts 0 lookups from C1/C2/C5 sites. Red today with the counts
      per site; it gets a tripwire run with one site put back.
    - Green: those counts at 0, the inventories' lookup lines for C1/C2/C5 gone, the lock still in place.

S3. One wake per lane (L6), no lock around a signal (Mikhail on wait signals).
    exec_wake_locked, which signals every worker after any turn, goes. A turn's end wakes only the lanes it readied,
    through each readied Message's own wake primitive. exec_ready signals without taking the lock.
    - Red: two mapped workers A and B. Readying A must not wake B. A test-build counter of wake returns that took
      nothing is nonzero for B today (run_one 2587 wakes all).
    - Green: B's empty-wake count at 0 over the same run; the executor selftest's wait tests green.

S4. The family tree and the mapping written only on the owner's lane (L2, L3), then read without the lock.
    Each tree cell and mapping cell has one writer by decision 18's ownership list (1)-(3), Mikhail's own
    clarification: the parent for its child chain and the mapping, the Message itself for its own cells. A cell with
    one writer and a reader on the same lane needs no lock. Cross-lane readers read only single-writer control flags,
    atomic cells whose order does not matter (Mikhail).
    Depends on 5.6 (a capability after its target closes) for C3/C4, and on N's per-query decision.
    - Red: 57's tripwire on every tree and mapping write, run over the executor selftest and model tests. Red where
      a write happens off the owner's lane today: endp_try_retire unlinking from any lane that drops the last
      reference, orphan_children's re-rooting, the host arms of mapping_authority.
    - Green: the tripwire green, then the lock calls around L2/L3 removed, the UAF kit clean.

S5. The runtime-level lists (L5). Waits on 5.1.
    rt->slots, rt->n, the retire queue, the reap queue, rt->transport and e->ui_lane each either go or become a cell in
    a Message's arena, by Mikhail's answer to 5.1. With "the executor frees its own memory", reap and join go: each
    worker frees its own wait state and ends. LmxMsgCtxPack, allocated on the parent's lane and freed by the child's
    thread (alloc inventory 2868/2993/3086), becomes the child's own first state.
    - Red: per list, a test that drops a Message's last reference on a lane other than its parent's, and checks with
      the tripwire that its storage is freed by its parent's settle write on the parent's lane.

S6. The lock deleted (L4, L7, L8 and what remains).
    - Red: lmx_msg_exec_lock and unlock defined to abort() in a test build. Every remaining call site is red by name
      in the executor selftest and model tests.
    - Green: grep -c lmx_msg_exec_lock at 0 in exec.c, lm1 and lm2, and the host lock gone. The only lock left is
      m->mail, the message collection's (lm1/lm2 mail_lock 6+6, exec.c mailbox functions). The gates and the UAF kit
      are green.

A1-A3. The arena (19.29.2), after S6 or interleaved where a stage touches the same record. Waits on 5.4.
    A1. The Message's control record (LmxMsg, today calloc in slot_new 418) becomes a Structure in its own arena,
        created on the launching parent's lane; lmx_sched_record and lmx_root_record already follow that pattern.
    A2. The arena's bookkeeping (LmxOwnedRange and LmxMsgBlock records, calloc at 652/690/737/780/3633/3698) and the
        Message's own arrays (path, init, done_*, roots) are allocated from that arena.
    A3. An admitted copy's arena (5.4). Mailbox entries (LmxMsgCopy) follow his answer.
    - Red for each: the balance test's two-cycle live count (ld --wrap) with the record's allocation counted as
      heap. It is red while any such record comes from malloc, and green once the arena's blocks are the only heap
      allocations.
