Runtime merge: the helper and what its tests actually prove
============================================================
Fable lane, 2026-09-12, ticket 134100. Branch fable/merge from main 9f71111f.

THE SHAPE

    lmx_merge_owned(operands, count, body, container,
                    src_ranges, eternal_ranges, dst_chars,
                    dst_blocks, dst_ranges, out)

Operands are live Structures, left to right. The result is a THIRD independent
Structure whose direct slots are the copies of each operand's direct CHILDREN,
operand by operand and child by child, then the body's fields. The operand
ROOTS are never slots, which is what makes the result one flat tree rather than
a container of containers, and therefore why a copied method is invoked through
the result.

ONE lmx_graph_copy_many_owned call covers every ordinary operand root and the
body together, so one map spans the whole operation: an object reached from two
operands is copied once, cross-operand aliases and cycles keep their shape, and
copied node links point at copied ancestors or at zero. METHOD descriptors and
admitted eternal branches stay terminals by address.

An eternal operand is NOT handed to the copier, which refuses an eternal root
outright. Its own direct child references are taken as terminals instead. The
retention array is never imported.

Atomic: the copies, the result root and its slots are built under private
block/range heads and moved in only on complete success. On any failure out
stays null, the destination heads are untouched, sources are unchanged and every
private allocation is released.

A DECISION I HAD TO MAKE, recorded because it is not in the ticket

The container that becomes result.node is validated against the DESTINATION
ranges, not the source ranges. The result lives in the destination, and a
Structure must not point its node at another owner's storage. For an ordinary
merge inside one Message the two are the same list and the distinction is
invisible; it only bites when a merge writes into a different owner, and there a
container from the old owner is a defect rather than a convenience. My first
version validated against the source and my own test caught it: merging a merge
result failed, because that result carried a node into the previous owner.

TWO BUGS MY OWN TEST FOUND, both worth keeping in mind

1. A phantom leak. The helper allocated its scratch arrays with calloc and freed
   them with free. The instrumented test builds redefine malloc and free as a
   PAIR, so a calloc'd block freed by the counted free decremented a counter it
   never incremented, and every failure position reported a negative delta. Now
   malloc plus an explicit memset. The lesson is narrow but sharp: under a
   redefined allocator, mixing calloc with free is not merely untidy, it reports
   false results.
2. The cross-owner node link above.

WHAT THE SELFTEST PROVES, 243 checks

  two operands and a body, and the exact field order;
  operand roots never appear as slots, checked slot by slot;
  a target reached from BOTH operands copied once;
  two distinct cells holding the same value staying distinct;
  a used lexical ancestor copied with node fixups down to zero;
  a cycle resolving to the copy;
  Array record and backing copied;
  METHOD shared by pointer, across two merges;
  an admitted eternal operand used by address while an ordinary operand in the
    same merge is copied;
  a merge result used as a later merge operand;
  result.node from the container, and zero when none is given;
  sources unchanged, and a write to the result not reaching them;
  six refusals, each leaving out null;
  a 200-field operand, which rejects any hidden cap;
  allocation failure at all 43 positions, then success at 44, with nothing
    admitted, nothing leaked and no source touched at any position.
